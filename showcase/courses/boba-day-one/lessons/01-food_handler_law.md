# Who is a food handler under FHR 2009

> [!info] Stuff to memorise · 20 min · study Sun 11 Oct
> You will be able to say whether you are a food handler and name what the Food Hygiene Regulations 2009 require of you before your first shift. Covers reg. 2's definition — anyone preparing food, touching food or food-contact surfaces, or handling packaged food or appliances — plus the training duty in regs. 30 and 31(1). Prevents assuming the SOP folder is the only rule that applies behind the bar.

## Explainer

The SOP folder tells you how Mutiara makes drinks. It does not tell you who the law counts as a food handler, or what you must have done before your first shift. Those come from the Food Hygiene Regulations 2009, P.U.(A) 95/2009, made under the Food Act 1983.

Regulation 2 defines a food handler as anyone who is directly involved in preparing food, comes into contact with food or food-contact surfaces, or handles packaged or unpackaged food or appliances in food premises. Your one-page summary puts it plainly: crew who scoop pearls, pour milk, seal cups or wash shakers are food handlers. That is the whole bar team, including whoever is on the sealer and whoever is at the sink.

The word doing the work is *contact*. You do not have to cook anything to be covered. Touching a shaker, a sealing head, a scoop or a cup rim is contact with a food-contact surface. Handling a sealed cup or a bag of dry pearls is handling packaged or unpackaged food. The definition is about what you touch, not about your job title.

Once you are a food handler, regs. 30 and 31(1) attach duties before your first shift. You attend food handler training and get a Certificate of Food Handlers Training from an institution specified by the Director, MOH-recognised. You go for a medical check and vaccination by a registered medical practitioner — in practice, the anti-typhoid injection. The owner must not let you work without training, medical examination and vaccination, and must keep those records for inspection.

Reg. 31(2) covers illness: if you have, carry, or may have a food-borne disease, do not enter the food premises or handle food, report it to management immediately, and stay off work until a registered medical practitioner certifies you cured and fit to work.

The penalties are real. Most food-handler offences under regs. 30, 31 and 33–38 carry a fine of up to RM 10,000 or imprisonment of up to 2 years. The clothing offence under reg. 32 carries a fine of up to RM 1,000. The summary is not legal advice and the regulations may have been amended since 2009 — check the current text with the Food Safety and Quality Division, MOH.

## Four Mutiara roles, checked against reg. 2

**Role A — pearl station.** You scoop cooked pearls from the warmer into cups. You are directly involved in preparing food and you contact the pearls and the scoop. Food handler under reg. 2. Reg. 30 and 31(1) apply before your first shift.

**Role B — sealer and cup runner.** You take the filled cup, wipe the rim and run the sealer. You never touch the drink itself, but you contact the cup rim and the sealing head — both food-contact surfaces — and you handle the packaged cup. Food handler under reg. 2. Same duties.

**Role C — shaker wash-up.** You rinse and sanitise shakers between orders. You contact a food-contact surface. Food handler under reg. 2. Same duties.

**Role D — cashier.** You take payment and hand over the sealed cup. Handing over a sealed cup is handling packaged food, and the cashier also answers allergen questions and prints the sticker. Food handler under reg. 2. Same duties — the summary's definition includes anyone who handles packaged or unpackaged food in food premises.

What the four cases have in common: the test is contact, not cooking. If the answer to "do I touch food or something food touches?" is yes, regs. 30 and 31(1) apply to you.

## A common mix-up

> [!failure] The belief
> The SOP folder is the only rule that applies behind the bar — if I follow the SOP, I am compliant.

**Why it seems right.** The SOP is what you are trained on, it is written for this shop, and it covers the drinks, the cleaning and the closing routine. It reads like the complete rulebook because it is the only document anyone hands you.

> [!success] What is true
> The SOP is the shop's operating procedure. The Food Hygiene Regulations 2009 sit above it as law, and they impose duties the SOP may not repeat — reg. 30 training and the Certificate of Food Handlers Training, reg. 31(1) medical examination and vaccination, reg. 31(2) staying off work when ill, reg. 32 clothing, reg. 33 personal hygiene. The difference it predicts: a shop with a perfect SOP and an untrained, unvaccinated crew member is still in breach, and most food-handler offences carry a fine of up to RM 10,000 or imprisonment up to 2 years.

## Key points

- Reg. 2 defines a food handler by contact: preparing food, touching food or food-contact surfaces, or handling packaged or unpackaged food or appliances in food premises.
- Scooping pearls, pouring milk, sealing cups and washing shakers all fall inside the reg. 2 definition — the whole bar team, not just whoever cooks.
- Reg. 30 requires food handler training and a Certificate of Food Handlers Training from an institution specified by the Director.
- Reg. 31(1) requires medical examination and vaccination by a registered medical practitioner, and the owner must not let you work without all three and must keep the records.
- Reg. 31(2) requires you to stay out of the premises and report immediately if you have, carry, or may have a food-borne disease, until a registered medical practitioner certifies you fit.
- Most food-handler offences under regs. 30, 31 and 33–38 carry a fine up to RM 10,000 or imprisonment up to 2 years; the reg. 32 clothing offence carries a fine up to RM 1,000.

## Check yourself

Answer from the reg. 2 definition and the duties in regs. 30 and 31.

> [!question] Q1 · mcq
> A new crew member at Mutiara spends the whole shift at the sealer: they take the filled cup, wipe the rim, run the sealing head and pass the sealed cup to the counter. They never touch the drink itself. Under reg. 2 of the Food Hygiene Regulations 2009, what is their status?
>
> - a) Not a food handler, because they never touch the drink or any unwrapped food.
> - b) A food handler, because they contact food-contact surfaces and handle packaged food.
> - c) A food handler only once they have attended the MOH food handler course.
> - d) Not a food handler, because sealing is packaging, and reg. 2 covers only preparation.

> [!success]- Answer
> b) A food handler, because they contact food-contact surfaces and handle packaged food.
>
> Reg. 2 covers anyone in contact with food or food-contact surfaces, or handling packaged or unpackaged food or appliances. The cup rim and sealing head are food-contact surfaces and the sealed cup is packaged food, so the sealer is a food handler. Training is a duty that follows from being a food handler, not the thing that makes you one.

> A crew member tells you they have been vomiting since last night and their shift starts in two hours. Which of the following does reg. 31(2) require of them? (Select all that apply.)

> - c) Stay off work until a registered medical practitioner certifies them cured and fit to work.
