# Building a drink to the sticker in under 90 seconds

> [!info] A skill you do · 30 min · study Fri 16 Oct
> You will be able to read a cup sticker and build the drink it describes, inside the SOP's 90-second target. Covers BS01 step by step in a 500 ml regular or 700 ml large cup, syrup and ice by the sticker's percentages, sealing, and checking the finished cup against the sticker before it reaches the counter. Prevents remakes caused by building from memory instead of from the sticker.

## Explainer

At Mutiara Tea Bar, a drink is not built from memory. It is built from the sticker. Section 6.4 of your SOP opens with the rule that decides everything after it: **read the sticker first**. The sticker carries five things you must transfer into the cup — size, sweetness, ice, milk type, toppings. Miss one and the drink is a remake.

The most serious miss is milk type. If the sticker says **OAT** and you pour fresh milk, an allergy customer drinks dairy. Section 6.4 step 1 puts that in plain words: wrong milk for an allergy customer is the most serious mistake in this shop.

The second rule is order. Syrup goes in before ice. Ice before syrup washes the stripes off the cup wall and you cannot get them back. Brown sugar drinks are never shaken at the bar — the customer shakes, after they leave.

The target is **under 90 seconds per drink**. That is not a speed contest; it is a rhythm. Read, scoop, stripe, ice, milk, tea, seal, hand over. Each step has one thing that goes wrong, and the SOP names it next to the step so you can catch it in the second it happens.

Your cup sizes are fixed: **500 ml regular** and **700 ml large**. Your milk volumes are fixed: **160 ml regular, 220 ml large**. Tea: **100 ml regular, 140 ml large**. Pearls: **1 scoop (60 g) regular, 1.5 scoops (90 g) large**. Only the size letter changes these numbers; sweetness, ice and milk type come from the rest of the sticker.

## Step by step

This is BS01 Brown Sugar Milk Tea, built to a sticker that reads: **BS01-L 75% LI OAT**. Large cup, 75% sweetness, less ice, oat milk. Follow the eight steps in this order. The order is forced — syrup before ice, milk before tea — and reversing any pair changes the drink.

### 1. Read the sticker out loud

Say the five fields to yourself before you touch anything: size (R or L), sweetness (%), ice level, milk type (fresh or OAT), toppings. Scan for **ALG**. For `BS01-L 75% LI OAT` you say: large, 75%, less ice, oat, pearls.

> [!warning]
> Building from memory. The SOP states this is the most serious mistake in the shop — wrong milk for an allergy customer.

> [!check] Check
> You can say all five fields without looking at the sticker again.

### 2. Scoop the pearls

Use the slotted pearl spoon. Large cup: **1.5 scoops = 90 g**. Regular cup: **1 scoop = 60 g**. Drop them into the cup first.

> [!warning]
> Bare hands on food is an offence under [LAW, reg. 36(1)(e)]. Too few pearls is the top customer complaint.

> [!check] Check
> The pearls are in the cup: 90 g for large, 60 g for regular.

### 3. Stripe the cup with warm syrup

Take the brown sugar syrup bottle **from the warm water bath on the bar** (or refill it if empty). Add the syrup for 75% sweetness: section 7 gives 5 pumps (50 ml) for a large cup, 3 for a regular. Tilt the cup and turn it so the syrup coats the inside wall — that is the tiger stripe.

> [!warning]
> Cold or thin syrup will not stick. No stripes. The SOP says warm syrup sticks to the cup wall, cold syrup slides off.

> [!check] Check
> You can see brown streaks holding on the inside wall when you hold the cup up.

### 4. Add ice for the sticker's ice level

Use the ice scoop. `LI` means less ice: 1 scoop (85 g) in a large cup, half a scoop (60 g) in a regular one (section 7). Drop ice in **after** the syrup.

> [!warning]
> Ice before syrup washes the stripes away. Syrup always goes in first.

> [!check] Check
> Stripes are still visible through the ice.

### 5. Pour the milk slowly over the ice

Large cup: **220 ml**. Regular cup: **160 ml**. This sticker says **OAT**, so pour oat milk — not fresh. Pour slowly, over the ice, not down the side.

> [!warning]
> Pouring fast melts the stripes. Wrong milk type can cause an allergic reaction.

> [!check] Check
> Milk level sits above the ice, stripes still readable on the wall.

### 6. Top with black tea over the back of a spoon

Large cup: **140 ml**. Regular cup: **100 ml**. Pour slowly over the back of a spoon so the tea floats as a layer on top of the milk.

> [!warning]
> Pouring fast mixes everything brown. Still drinkable, but customers photograph this drink.

> [!check] Check
> You can see three bands: dark tea on top, milk in the middle, syrup stripes on the wall.

### 7. Wipe the rim, seal, and invert over the sink

Wipe the cup rim dry with a clean cloth. Seal with **one full press** of the handle. Turn the cup upside down over the sink for **2 seconds**.

> [!warning]
> A wet rim or crooked film leaks. The SOP says this is how most counter spills start.

> [!check] Check
> No drip in the two seconds upside down. If it drips, re-seal before it reaches the counter.

### 8. Stick the sticker on and hand over

Press the sticker onto the cup. Give a **12 mm wide straw** — a thin straw blocks on the pearls. Read the order number aloud, then say the sweetness and milk type: "Large brown sugar, 75 percent, oat milk." Tell the customer: **shake before drinking**.

> [!warning]
> Shaking at the bar ruins the stripes. The customer shakes, not you.

> [!check] Check
> Sticker is on, straw is wide, order number has been said out loud.

## Checklist

- [ ] Sticker read: size, sweetness, ice, milk type, toppings — all five said out loud
  - *Prevents building from memory, which is how allergy drinks get made wrong*
- [ ] ALG checked on the sticker
  - *An allergy order is made with washed hands, new gloves, a shaker and scoop from the clean rack and fresh ice with the clean scoop (section 9).*
- [ ] Pearls scooped with the slotted spoon — 60 g regular, 90 g large
  - *Bare hands on food is an offence under [LAW, reg. 36(1)(e)]*
- [ ] Syrup taken from the warm water bath, not the shelf
  - *Cold syrup slides off the wall and you get no stripes*
- [ ] Syrup added before ice, cup tilted and turned to coat the wall
  - *Ice first washes the stripes away and they do not come back*
- [ ] Milk volume matched to size: 160 ml regular, 220 ml large
  - *Short pours get sent back; over-pours overflow at the seal*
- [ ] Milk type matches the sticker — OAT means oat, never fresh
  - *Wrong milk type can cause an allergic reaction*
- [ ] Tea poured over the back of a spoon so it floats as a layer
  - *Fast pouring mixes everything brown and loses the look customers photograph*
- [ ] Rim wiped dry, film sealed with one full press, cup inverted 2 seconds over the sink
  - *A wet rim or crooked film leaks — this is how most counter spills start*
- [ ] Sticker on, 12 mm straw in, order number read aloud, shake-before-drinking told to the customer
  - *A thin straw blocks on the pearls and the customer blames the drink*

## Two stickers, two builds — where they diverge

**Sticker A: `BS01-R 50% RI`** — regular, 50% sweetness, normal ice, fresh milk.

- Pearls: 1 scoop = 60 g
- Syrup: 50% = 2 pumps, from the warm bath, tilted to coat
- Ice: RI = 1 scoop (120 g), after syrup
- Milk: 160 ml fresh, poured slowly over the ice
- Tea: 100 ml black, over the back of a spoon
- Seal, invert 2 s, sticker on, 12 mm straw, order number read aloud

**Sticker B: `BS01-L 75% LI OAT`** — large, 75% sweetness, less ice, oat milk.

- Pearls: 1.5 scoops = 90 g
- Syrup: 75% = 5 pumps, warm, tilted to coat
- Ice: LI = 1 scoop (85 g), after syrup
- Milk: 220 ml oat, poured slowly over the ice
- Tea: 140 ml black, over the back of a spoon
- Seal, invert 2 s, sticker on, 12 mm straw, order number read aloud

The steps are identical. The values change: pearl scoops, pumps, ice, milk volume and type, and tea volume. Everything else — the order, the tilt, the slow pour, the seal — is the same on both. That is why the 90-second target is reachable: you are not learning two drinks, you are reading two stickers.

## A common mix-up

> [!failure] The belief
> The stripes are decoration. If I'm rushing, I can skip the tilt-and-turn and just pour the syrup straight in.

**Why it seems right.** The drink still tastes like brown sugar milk tea. The customer drinks it, nobody complains, and you saved four seconds on a busy shift.

> [!success] What is true
> The stripes are the product. Section 6.4 step 3 exists because warm syrup coating the cup wall is what makes this drink BS01 and not a plain milk tea with syrup stirred in. The SOP also tells the customer to shake before drinking — the shake is what mixes the stripes into the drink. If you skip the stripe, there is nothing to shake and the customer gets a flat brown cup. The 90 seconds is a rhythm, not a reason to drop a step.

## Scenario

It is 7:40 pm, the queue is six deep, and you have three stickers on the rail. The third one reads `BS01-L 75% LI OAT`. You reach for the syrup bottle on the shelf because the warm bath bottle is empty and you do not want to refill it mid-rush.

**The warm bath bottle is empty. What do you do?**

- Use the shelf bottle — it's the same syrup, just cooler.
- Refill the warm bath bottle from the squeeze bottle, wait for it to come up to temperature, then build.
- Skip the stripes on this one and add extra syrup at the end to make up for it.

> [!example]- Outcomes
> - ✗ Use the shelf bottle — it's the same syrup, just cooler.: You tilt the cup and turn it. The syrup runs straight down to the pearls. When you hold the cup up, the wall is clean — no stripes. You have made a brown sugar milk tea with no tiger stripe.
> - ✓ Refill the warm bath bottle from the squeeze bottle, wait for it to come up to temperature, then build.: The syrup is warm again. You tilt, turn, and the wall holds a stripe. The drink takes about 20 seconds longer than target, but it is a BS01.
> - ✗ Skip the stripes on this one and add extra syrup at the end to make up for it.: Extra syrup pools at the bottom. The customer shakes it and gets a sweet first sip and a plain last third. The cup never had a stripe to photograph.

**You have the warm syrup and the stripes are on the wall. The sticker says `LI` and `OAT`. Ice or milk next?**

- Ice first — it chills the cup and the milk pours better over cold.
- Milk first — it protects the stripes from the ice.
- Ice next, at the less-ice level, then oat milk slowly over the ice.

> [!example]- Outcomes
> - ✗ Ice first — it chills the cup and the milk pours better over cold.: The ice hits the stripes and the syrup slides down the wall into the pearls. The stripes are gone. You now have a cup of pearls and cold syrup.
> - ✗ Milk first — it protects the stripes from the ice.: Milk goes in over the stripes and dilutes them before the ice arrives. The wall is milky brown, not striped.
> - ✓ Ice next, at the less-ice level, then oat milk slowly over the ice.: The ice sits on top of the stripes without disturbing them. The oat milk goes in at 220 ml, slowly, and the stripes are still readable on the wall.

**The cup is sealed and inverted clean over the sink. You are about to hand it over. What do you say?**

- Just the order number. The customer can see the sticker.
- Order number, then the sweetness and milk type, then tell them to shake before drinking.
- Shake it yourself at the bar before handing it over so it's ready to drink.

> [!example]- Outcomes
> - ✗ Just the order number. The customer can see the sticker.: The customer takes the cup, does not shake it, and drinks a layer of tea, then milk, then syrup. They come back asking what went wrong.
> - ✓ Order number, then the sweetness and milk type, then tell them to shake before drinking.: The customer shakes the cup at the counter, the stripes mix into the milk, and the first sip tastes the way BS01 is supposed to taste.
> - ✗ Shake it yourself at the bar before handing it over so it's ready to drink.: You shake it at the bar. The stripes are gone before the customer ever sees the cup. The SOP says shaking at the bar ruins the stripes — the customer shakes, not you.

> [!summary] Debrief
> Every wrong turn in this scenario shares one move: you treated a step as optional because the drink would still be drinkable. Cold syrup, ice before syrup, shaking at the bar — none of them make the drink unsafe, and all of them make it not a BS01. The SOP's 90-second target assumes every step happens. When you are rushing, the step you drop is the one that made the drink what the sticker says it is.
