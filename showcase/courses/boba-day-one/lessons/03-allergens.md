# Allergens: what the creamer really contains

> [!info] Get it right first time · 25 min · study Mon 12 Oct
> You will be able to answer any allergen question at the counter without guessing. Covers the creamer label's four ingredients — glucose syrup, vegetable oil, sodium caseinate (milk), soy lecithin — why "non-dairy creamer" is not dairy-free, and which menu items list milk and caffeine. Prevents the one wrong sentence that turns a milk allergy into an emergency.

## Explainer

A milk allergy is not a preference: section 9 lists trouble breathing among the reactions. Your creamer label is the single most dangerous object on the bar, because it says **non-dairy** and contains **milk**.

Section 9 of the Mutiara SOP lists the four ingredients on the creamer label:

| Ingredient | What it actually is |
|---|---|
| Glucose syrup | Sugar, no protein |
| Vegetable oil | Fat, no protein |
| **Sodium caseinate (milk)** | **A milk protein.** |
| Emulsifier (soy lecithin) | Soy-derived, a declared soy allergen |

"Non-dairy" on the tub is not a promise that the creamer is free of milk. This one contains sodium caseinate, so it is unsafe for a milk allergy. The SOP states it flatly: *"Non-dairy creamer" is NOT safe for a milk allergy. It contains milk protein. Never tell a customer that non-dairy means dairy-free.*

On this bar you also handle egg, soy and oats. Section 9 tells you to be honest about that: you cannot promise zero traces, so you tell the customer what you did and let them decide.

If someone reacts — swollen lips or face, trouble breathing, hives, vomiting — you call 999 at once, tell the shift lead, keep the person still, and ask whether they carry their own adrenaline auto-injector. You give them nothing to eat or drink.

## A common mix-up

> [!failure] The belief
> "Non-dairy creamer" means there is no milk in it, so classic milk tea is safe for a milk allergy.

**Why it seems right.** The word on the tub is literally "non-dairy". Every other food label you have ever read uses "non-" to mean "without", and the powder is white and sold beside the coffee, not in the chiller with the fresh milk.

> [!success] What is true
> The creamer label lists sodium caseinate (milk) as its third ingredient. Sodium caseinate is a milk protein, so a creamer sold as non-dairy can still be unsafe for a milk allergy. The SOP's own wording is that you must never tell a customer that non-dairy means dairy-free.

## Scenario

It is 18:40, the queue is eight deep, and you are alone on the POS while the bar works through a rush. A parent leans in and asks: "Is the classic milk tea dairy-free? My daughter is allergic to milk." The bar is loud, the shift lead is at the back restocking cups, and the parent is waiting for a yes or a no.

**What do you say first?**

- "Yes, we use non-dairy creamer, so it's dairy-free."
- "No — the classic milk tea contains milk protein. Our creamer lists sodium caseinate."
- "I'm not sure, let me check with the bar."

> [!example]- Outcomes
> - ✗ "Yes, we use non-dairy creamer, so it's dairy-free.": The parent orders it, the daughter drinks it, and within minutes her lips swell and she starts struggling to breathe. The creamer contains sodium caseinate, a milk protein. You have just told a parent that a milk-containing drink is milk-free.
> - ✓ "No — the classic milk tea contains milk protein. Our creamer lists sodium caseinate.": The parent stops and puts the menu down. You have given the honest answer, and the conversation can now move to what she can safely order.
> - ✗ "I'm not sure, let me check with the bar.": Checking beats guessing (section 1: if you are not sure, ask; never guess about allergens), so nobody is harmed. But this answer is in section 9 and on the allergen board: the creamer contains milk protein. Know it before the rush, so the parent gets the right answer at once.

**The parent asks what her daughter can drink. What do you offer?**

- "The fruit teas FT01, FT02 and FT03 have no milk in the recipe. I can make one with clean equipment, without cheese foam or pudding. We do handle milk on this bar, so I can't promise zero traces."
- "Anything on the menu is fine as long as I use non-dairy creamer."
- "I can make the classic milk tea with cheese foam on the side, that should be fine."

> [!example]- Outcomes
> - ✓ "The fruit teas FT01, FT02 and FT03 have no milk in the recipe. I can make one with clean equipment, without cheese foam or pudding. We do handle milk on this bar, so I can't promise zero traces.": The parent hears three safe options, hears the honest limit, and decides for herself. She orders FT02.
> - ✗ "Anything on the menu is fine as long as I use non-dairy creamer.": You have repeated the same false claim. The creamer is the source of the milk protein, so using it is exactly what makes the drink unsafe.
> - ✗ "I can make the classic milk tea with cheese foam on the side, that should be fine.": Cheese foam is dairy. You have just offered an allergy customer a second dairy topping, and the drink itself still contains caseinate.

**The parent orders FT02. What do you do before you hand the cup to the bar?**

- Say "ALLERGY: milk" out loud to the bar and add ALG to the sticker.
- Type the order quietly into the POS and let the bar read the screen.
- Tell the parent you will handle it personally and make the drink yourself without telling anyone.

> [!example]- Outcomes
> - ✓ Say "ALLERGY: milk" out loud to the bar and add ALG to the sticker.: The bar hears the call-out, sees ALG on the sticker, washes hands, pulls new gloves and a shaker and scoop from the clean rack, and uses fresh ice with the clean scoop. The drink goes out with no cheese foam and no pudding.
> - ✗ Type the order quietly into the POS and let the bar read the screen.: The bar is mid-rush and never looks at the screen. The drink is made on the same shaker as the last milk tea. Nobody in the chain knows an allergy order came through.
> - ✗ Tell the parent you will handle it personally and make the drink yourself without telling anyone.: You leave the POS, the queue stalls, and no one on the bar knows an allergy order is in progress. If the parent asks a follow-up while you are away, nobody can answer it.

> [!summary] Debrief
> Every wrong turn in this scenario comes from the same place: treating the word "non-dairy" as a medical guarantee instead of a butterfat label. The correct path is always the same three moves — say what is actually in the drink, offer the drinks that are clearly safe and name the trace limit, then put the allergy in the chain where the bar can see and hear it. Hesitating, guessing, or staying silent all leave the customer deciding on information you already had.

## Checklist

**Before you hand any allergy order to the bar**

- [ ] You have read the allergen board and section 8 with the customer, not for them.
  - *Prevents a guess about a topping you did not check.*
- [ ] You said the drink's milk status out loud in plain words — "contains milk protein" or "no milk in the recipe".
  - *Prevents the customer hearing "non-dairy" and assuming dairy-free.*
- [ ] You named the trace limit: we handle milk, egg, soy and oats on one small bar, so zero traces cannot be promised.
  - *Prevents a promise the shop cannot keep.*
- [ ] The cashier has said "ALLERGY: milk" out loud to the bar.
  - *Prevents the bar making the drink on shared equipment in a rush.*
- [ ] ALG is on the sticker and visible to whoever builds the drink.
  - *Prevents the allergy being lost between POS and bar.*
- [ ] Hands washed, new gloves on, shaker and scoop taken from the clean rack.
  - *Prevents milk residue from the previous drink entering the cup.*
- [ ] Fresh ice, taken with the clean scoop from the clean rack.
  - *Prevents cross-contact from a scoop that has touched milk drinks.*
- [ ] No cheese foam and no pudding on the order.
  - *Both are dairy and both are the easiest toppings to add by habit.*
- [ ] You told the customer what you did and let them decide.
  - *Prevents you making the safety decision on their behalf.*

> If the customer reacts — swollen lips or face, trouble breathing, hives, vomiting — stop the checklist. Call 999 at once, tell the shift lead, keep the person still, and ask if they carry their own adrenaline auto-injector. Give them nothing to eat or drink.

## Key points

- The creamer label lists four ingredients: glucose syrup, vegetable oil, sodium caseinate (milk), and emulsifier (soy lecithin).
- Sodium caseinate is a milk protein, so "non-dairy creamer" is not safe for a milk allergy and you must never say that non-dairy means dairy-free.
- The classic milk tea contains milk protein; the fruit teas FT01, FT02 and FT03 have no milk in the recipe.
- Every tea-based drink on the board carries caffeine; the brown sugar syrup and the toppings do not.
- A declared allergy is spoken out loud to the bar as "ALLERGY: milk" and marked ALG on the sticker before the drink is built.
- An allergy order is built with washed hands, new gloves, clean-rack shaker and scoop, fresh ice with the clean scoop, and no cheese foam or pudding.
- We handle milk, egg, soy and oats on one small bar, so you tell the customer what you did and let them decide rather than promising zero traces.
