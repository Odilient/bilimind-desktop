# Handwashing and hygiene rules you cannot skip

> [!info] Get it right first time · 25 min · study Sun 11 Oct
> You will be able to wash your hands for the full 20 seconds and name every trigger that sends you back to the sink. Covers soap and water before you start work, right after the toilet, and after raw food, rubbish or anything dirty, and the section 10 rule table with its source column. Prevents the two failures an inspector catches first: washing too briefly, and washing at the wrong moment.

## Explainer

Two hand-washing failures are invisible on a clean-looking bar: washing too briefly, and not going to the sink at the moment you were supposed to. The law sets the moments; the SOP sets the time.

Section 10 of the Mutiara SOP splits every hygiene rule into two columns: **[LAW]** and **[SHOP]**. The source column is not decoration. A [LAW] rule comes from Malaysia's Food Hygiene Regulations 2009 (FHR 2009) and carries a regulation number — reg. 33(1)(c), reg. 36(1)(e). A [SHOP] rule is ours. Break a [SHOP] rule and the shift lead corrects you. Break a [LAW] rule and the shop is exposed, not just you.

The hand rule itself is reg. 33(1)(c): **wash hands before starting work, right after using the toilet, and after handling raw food, rubbish or anything dirty.** Our house rule adds three more triggers: money, your phone, your face.

Twenty seconds is the number: the opening checklist says wash hands with soap for at least 20 seconds. Not "until it feels clean" — count it. Soap, running water, backs of hands, between fingers, thumbs, fingertips, wrists. Twenty seconds is roughly singing "Happy Birthday" twice through at normal speed.

Two rules sit next to the sink and are easy to skip. Take off your apron and cap **before** you go to the toilet (reg. 33(1)(b)) — the apron is what touches the drinks, and it does not go into the toilet with you. And cover any cut with a blue waterproof plaster, then a glove over it; blue because nothing in a milk tea is blue, so a lost plaster is visible in the cup.

## A common mix-up

> [!failure] The belief
> Twenty seconds is just a guideline — if my hands look clean and I used soap, I'm fine.

**Why it seems right.** Your hands do look clean after five seconds of soap. There is no visible difference between a five-second wash and a twenty-second one, so the clock feels like bureaucracy rather than hygiene.

> [!success] What is true
> Reg. 33(1)(c) requires the wash, and the SOP's opening checklist sets the standard for it: soap for at least 20 seconds. A five-second rinse fails that standard regardless of how the hands look. What twenty seconds buys you is the scrubbing of the areas a quick rinse never reaches: between fingers, thumbs, wrists and under the nails, where the residue that transfers into a drink actually sits. The difference predicts what reaches the cup: a five-second wash leaves what was on your hands, a counted twenty seconds removes it.

## Step by step

This is the wash you perform at the sink behind the bar. Twenty seconds, counted, every time one of the triggers fires.

### 1. Wet your hands and forearms under running water

Turn the tap on and let the water run over your hands and up past your wrists. Do not fill the basin and dip — running water carries residue away, standing water holds it.

> [!warning]
> Warm water is more comfortable but the temperature is not the point; running water is.

> [!check] Check
> Water is flowing out of the tap, not sitting in the basin.

### 2. Apply soap and start the count

One pump of liquid soap into your palm. Start counting twenty seconds now, not after you have finished lathering. Roughly two slow rounds of "Happy Birthday" at normal singing speed.

> [!warning]
> Do not start the count when you begin rinsing — that is the most common way a wash lands at eight seconds.

> [!check] Check
> You know roughly where you are in the count before you start scrubbing.

### 3. Scrub palms, backs of hands and between fingers

Rub palm to palm, then the back of each hand with the other palm, then interlace your fingers and work the soap between them. This is where the residue from money and phones sits.

> [!warning]
> Skipping the backs of the hands is the second-most-common miss after the thumbs.

> [!check] Check
> Soap is visible between every pair of fingers.

### 4. Scrub thumbs, fingertips and wrists

Rotate each thumb inside the opposite palm. Rub your fingertips into your palms to work under the nails. Finish with each wrist.

> [!warning]
> Fingernails must be short and clean (reg. 33(1)(a)); no nail polish or false nails is our [SHOP] rule. Long nails trap what the scrub cannot reach.

> [!check] Check
> All four areas — thumbs, fingertips, nails, wrists — have been scrubbed.

### 5. Rinse under running water

Rinse from wrists down to fingertips so the soap and the residue travel off your hands, not back up your arms.

> [!warning]
> Rinsing upward pushes dirty water toward your wrists.

> [!check] Check
> No soap film remains on your hands.

### 6. Dry with a paper towel and use it to turn off the tap

Dry your hands fully on a paper towel, then use that same towel to grip the tap handle and turn it off. Bin the towel.

> [!warning]
> Bare hands on the tap handle re-contaminate the hands you just washed — the handle was touched before the wash.

> [!check] Check
> The tap is off and your hands did not touch the handle bare.

## Scenario

It is 7:40 pm on a Friday. The queue is eight deep, you are the only one on the bar, and the brown sugar milk teas are backed up. You just took a fifty-ringgit note from the customer in front of you and handed back change. The next order is a BS01 and the pearls are already scooped into the cup.

**You have the customer's cash in your hand and the next cup waiting. What do you do?**

- Finish the drink first, then wash your hands once the queue clears.
- Wash your hands now, then build the drink.
- Wipe your hands on your apron and build the drink.

> [!example]- Outcomes
> - ✗ Finish the drink first, then wash your hands once the queue clears.: You hand over the drink. The shift lead sees your bare hand on the cup lid straight after the cash and stops you. The whole queue watches you walk to the sink and start again from step one of the wash — twenty seconds you could have spent before the cup.
> - ✓ Wash your hands now, then build the drink.: You put the cash in the drawer, step to the sink and count twenty seconds. The customer waits a few seconds longer. The cup is built with clean hands and the shift lead says nothing, because there is nothing to say.
> - ✗ Wipe your hands on your apron and build the drink.: The apron is now the contaminated surface, and it hangs over the counter for the rest of the shift. You have moved the problem from your hands to the garment that touches every drink you make tonight.

**Mid-build, you slice your finger on the sealer handle. It is a small cut but it is bleeding. What do you do?**

- Wrap it in a tissue, put the apron over it, and keep going.
- Cover it with a blue waterproof plaster, then wear a glove over the plaster.
- Keep the finger out of the way and finish the queue before dealing with it.

> [!example]- Outcomes
> - ✗ Wrap it in a tissue, put the apron over it, and keep going.: The tissue is white and the apron is not waterproof. Ten minutes later there is no tissue on your finger and you cannot say where it went. Every cup made since then is suspect.
> - ✓ Cover it with a blue waterproof plaster, then wear a glove over the plaster.: The blue plaster is visible against everything in the shop, so if it ever comes loose you will see it in the cup. The glove over it keeps the plaster dry and in place through the rest of the shift.
> - ✗ Keep the finger out of the way and finish the queue before dealing with it.: You finish the queue. Blood has reached the cup rim on at least one drink, and the shop now has a product it cannot sell and a customer it cannot call back.

**The bin behind the bar is full. You tie the rubbish bag and carry it to the back. Do you wash your hands before you go back to the bar?**

- No — I only touched the outside of the bag.
- Yes — handling rubbish is one of the listed triggers.

> [!example]- Outcomes
> - ✗ No — I only touched the outside of the bag.: You go straight back to the bar. Rubbish is named in the hand-wash rule itself (reg. 33(1)(c)): the outside of a rubbish bag is exactly the kind of thing that sends you to the sink. The next cup you touch carries it.
> - ✓ Yes — handling rubbish is one of the listed triggers.: Twenty seconds at the sink, then back to the bar. Section 10 lists rubbish next to the toilet and raw food: wash before you touch anything behind the bar again.

**It is 9:55 pm. You need the toilet before closing. Your apron is on and your cap is on.**

- Go now, come back, wash hands, carry on.
- Take off the apron and cap, leave them in the locker, then go.
- Go now and wash the apron when you get back.

> [!example]- Outcomes
> - ✗ Go now, come back, wash hands, carry on.: You walk into the toilet wearing the apron that hangs over the drinks and the counter. Reg. 33(1)(b) requires it off before you go, and the apron has now been somewhere the shop's food must never be.
> - ✓ Take off the apron and cap, leave them in the locker, then go.: Apron and cap go in the locker. You use the toilet, come back, wash for twenty seconds, and put the apron and cap back on. Nothing that touches a drink left the shop floor.
> - ✗ Go now and wash the apron when you get back.: You cannot wash an apron in the twenty seconds before closing, and a wet apron is worse than a dry one. The rule was never about how the apron looks — it is about where the apron has been.

> [!summary] Debrief
> Every wrong turn in this shift shares one shape: the shortcut was faster, and the cost landed on somebody who was not in the room — the customer who drank from the cup, or the shop that took the write-up. The triggers are not a list to memorise for the quiz. They are the moments where the queue is loudest and the wash feels most optional, which is exactly why the SOP writes them down.

## Checklist

**Before you touch a cup**

- [ ] Fingernails are short and clean, with no polish and no false nails.
  - *reg. 33(1)(a) says short and clean; no polish or false nails is our [SHOP] rule.*
- [ ] No jewellery, watch, pin or accessory on your hands or wrists.
  - *reg. 33(1)(f) — accessories are surfaces that never get washed.*
- [ ] Apron and cap are on, and both came out of your locker this shift.
  - *reg. 32(2) — they are worn only inside the shop.*
- [ ] Apron pockets are empty.
  - *reg. 33(1)(e) — anything in a pocket can fall into a drink.*
- [ ] You have washed your hands for a counted twenty seconds since clocking in.
  - *reg. 33(1)(c) — wash before starting work; the opening checklist sets at least 20 seconds with soap.*
- [ ] Any cut is covered with a blue waterproof plaster and a glove over it.
  - *blue shows up in a cup; a bare plaster does not stay put.*
- [ ] Your phone is in the locker, not in your pocket.
  - *[SHOP] — a phone in a pocket is a phone you will touch, and touching it sends you back to the sink.*
- [ ] You know where the soap and paper towels are, and both are stocked.
  - *a sink with no soap is a wash you cannot perform.*

> [!note]
> Run this once at the start of every shift. It takes under a minute and it is the difference between a spot check that ends in a nod and one that ends in a write-up.
