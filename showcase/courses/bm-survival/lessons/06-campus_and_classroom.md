# On campus and in your BMK class

> [!info] Said out loud · 15 min · study Fri 16 Oct
> You will be able to introduce yourself to a lecturer, find the library and the admin office, and stop a class from running straight past you. Covers Saya pelajar antarabangsa, Di mana perpustakaan?, Saya tak faham, Boleh ulang? and minta maaf. Prevents sitting through a whole BMK session without ever saying you did not follow.

## Explainer

On the Grab ride in you learned to say *Saya nak pergi ke Taylor's*. That gets you to the gate. It does not get you through a lecture.

The gap is not vocabulary. It is that a BMK class moves at the speed of the room, and if you never say anything, the tutor will keep going and you will sit through the whole class understanding nothing. Three phrases stop that:

- **Saya tak faham.** — I don't understand.
- **Boleh ulang?** — Can you repeat that?
- **Boleh cakap perlahan sikit?** — Can you speak a bit slower?

Your phrasebook lists all three in its basics table, and they are the ones that keep a class from running past you. A tutor who hears *Saya tak faham* will rephrase. A tutor who hears nothing assumes you followed.

Two habits make these work. First, **Minta maaf** before the request — it is more polite than plain *maaf*, and you are interrupting. Second, say the phrase even when you half-understood. Half-understanding is the state you are trying to escape, and it looks identical to full understanding from the front of the room.

For the lecturer, one sentence carries the whole introduction: **Saya pelajar antarabangsa.** Add where you are from and what you study and you are done — *Saya dari Uzbekistan. Saya belajar sains komputer.*

For buildings, the pattern is **Di mana** plus the place. *Di mana perpustakaan?* works for the library, the admin office, the toilet. For class timing, **Bila kelas mula?**

And when a word goes past you, do not nod. Ask **Apa maksud "tugasan"?** — *tugasan* is assignment, and it will appear on every brief you get this semester.

## Talk it through

It is the first week of your BMK class. The tutor has just explained the assessment structure at speed, in Malay, and you caught about a third of it. Class is still running. You need to stop it from running past you — without derailing the whole room.

You are the pelajar antarabangsa; the other person is the pensyarah (BMK tutor). Goal: Get the tutor to slow down or repeat, and find out what one word you missed means — using Malay, in front of the class.

## Step by step

How to actually do this in the room. The order matters: you apologise, you name the problem, you make one specific request. Do not stack three requests at once — you will get three answers at speed and be back where you started.

### 1. Open with Minta maaf

Raise your hand slightly and say **Minta maaf.** Your phrasebook marks this as more polite than plain *maaf* — you are interrupting a class, so use the polite one.

> [!warning]
> Do not start with *Saya tak faham* alone. Without the apology it reads as a flat statement of fact rather than a request for help.

> [!check] Check
> The tutor's eyes are on you and they have stopped talking.

### 2. Name the problem in one sentence

Say **Saya tak faham.** Nothing else. This is the single most useful sentence in the room and it is complete on its own.

> [!warning]
> Do not add an explanation in English. The moment you switch to English the tutor may switch too, and you lose the practice.

> [!check] Check
> The tutor acknowledges you — usually a nod, or *oh, tak faham*.

### 3. Make one specific request

Pick exactly one: **Boleh ulang?** if you missed the content, or **Boleh cakap perlahan sikit?** if you heard it but could not keep up. Say it once and stop.

> [!warning]
> Asking both in a row gets you a fast repeat, which is the original problem.

> [!check] Check
> The tutor repeats or slows down, and you catch more the second time.

### 4. Ask about the one word you missed

If a single word is the blocker, name it: **Apa maksud "tugasan"?** The pattern is *Apa maksud* plus the word. *Tugasan* means assignment — your phrasebook lists it alongside *kuiz* and *peperiksaan*.

> [!warning]
> Substituting an English word for the Malay one defeats the exercise. Ask about the Malay word you heard.

> [!check] Check
> You can say what the word means in English without looking it up.

### 5. Close with Terima kasih

Say **Terima kasih** when you have your answer. It tells the tutor the problem is solved and the class can move on.

> [!warning]
> Do not just go quiet: without the thank-you the tutor cannot tell whether you got what you needed.

> [!check] Check
> The tutor moves on and you are not dreading the next session.

## Two students, same lecture, different outcome

The tutor says, at speed: *Tugasan kumpulan perlu dihantar sebelum kelas minggu depan.*

**Student A** catches *tugasan*, *kumpulan*, *kelas*, *minggu depan* — enough to guess there is a group assignment due next week. She nods. She does not know whether it is before the class or after, does not know the group, and does not know what *dihantar* means. She finds out on the due date that it had to be submitted online by 5pm on Sunday.

**Student B** raises a hand and says:

> Minta maaf. Saya tak faham. Boleh cakap perlahan sikit?

The tutor repeats it slower. B still misses *dihantar*. So:

> Apa maksud "dihantar"?

The tutor says *submit* and adds, in English, that it goes through the portal. B writes it down.

Same class. A leaves with a guess. B leaves with a deadline, a meaning and a submission method — and the tutor now knows B's name and that B asks.

## A common mix-up

> [!failure] The belief
> If I say *Saya tak faham* in class, the tutor will think I am not trying and mark me down.

**Why it seems right.** Everyone else in the room seems to be nodding, and saying you are lost out loud feels like admitting you should not be there.

> [!success] What is true
> A tutor cannot teach someone who has silently stopped following, and *Saya tak faham* is a request, not a confession — the phrasebook puts it in the same list as *Boleh ulang?* and *Boleh cakap perlahan sikit?*, all of which are things you say to get help. The tutor's problem is not that you are lost; it is that they cannot see it. Nodding through a session produces a learner who fails the quiz and never gave the tutor a chance to fix it.
